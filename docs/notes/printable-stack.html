01

The finding

Your collection is strong where most people are weak and empty where most people are strong. You have two serious code-CAD authoring tools, three image-to-3D generators, a headless viewer and a mesh processor. What you do not have is anything that checks a model, repairs it, or slices it. The front of the pipeline is well covered; the back half does not exist.

That matters because the back half is where printability is decided. A generative mesh that renders beautifully is routinely non-manifold, self-intersecting, has no flat base and carries features below nozzle width. Nothing in your current set would tell you that before the print fails.

The principle the whole stack rests on: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini cannot produce good mesh geometry directly — vertex soup is the one thing they are reliably bad at. They are excellent at writing code that produces geometry.

So the model never emits an STL. It emits a .scad or a Python script, you execute it, render it, show the render back to the model, assert on the mesh, and slice it. The quality of your output is set almost entirely by how tight that feedback loop is — not by which model you picked.

Constraints to put in the prompt

A model will not infer these. Stating them in the system prompt removes most unprintable output before the first render. Figures assume a 0.4 mm nozzle on FDM.

Minimum wall
0.8–1.2 mm · 2–3 perimeters
Unsupported overhang
≤ 45° · 55° with cooling
Unsupported bridge
5–10 mm
Smallest feature
0.4 mm wide · 0.3 deep
Clearance, loose fit
0.3 mm · press 0.1
Minimum hole
2 mm · prints ~0.15 under
Bottom-edge chamfer
0.2 mm · elephant's foot
Resin hollowing
2 mm wall · 2× 3 mm drains
Layer height
0.2 mm · fine 0.08
Build volume
256³ mm · split beyond
  • Always require a flat base. Generative models almost never produce one; it is the most common single cause of a failed art print.
  • Orient for layer-line strength. Parts are weakest across layers — state which axis takes load.
  • Split beyond bed size with registration features, not a bare plane cut. BOSL2 has dovetails and pins for exactly this.
